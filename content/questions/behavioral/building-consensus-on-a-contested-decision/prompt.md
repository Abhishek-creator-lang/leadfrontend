> "Tell me about a time a group was split on an important technical decision. How did you get everyone to a place where they could commit?"

Leads frequently face decisions where smart people disagree and there is no clearly right answer. This question tests whether you can run a fair process, separate facts from preferences, and reach a decision that people support even when it was not their first choice.

## What the interviewer is assessing

- **Facilitation**: structuring the discussion so it converges instead of going in circles.
- **Fairness**: making sure quieter or more junior voices were heard, not only the loudest.
- **Evidence**: using prototypes, data or criteria rather than opinion.
- **Decision rights**: knowing when consensus is not possible and who then decides.
- **Commitment**: how you made sure people who disagreed still committed.

## Common variants

- "Describe a time you had to get alignment among people with strong opinions."
- "How do you make decisions when your team disagrees?"
- "Tell me about a time you used 'disagree and commit'."
- "How do you resolve a technical debate that has been going on too long?"
