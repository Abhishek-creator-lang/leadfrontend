> "Pick one project you led from the very beginning to the end. What was the problem, how did you plan and run it, and what happened in the end?"

This is the anchor question of most lead-level loops. Interviewers often spend 20 minutes or more on it and drill into every layer: why the project existed, how you shaped it, how you handled the people involved and whether the result mattered.

## What the interviewer is assessing

- **Scope**: was this a team-sized or multi-team effort, and did you own the outcome or only a slice of the code?
- **Problem framing**: can you explain why the work mattered to users or the business before describing what you built?
- **Planning and sequencing**: milestones, de-risking, how you split work across people.
- **Technical judgement**: the key decisions and the alternatives you rejected.
- **Leading people**: delegation, unblocking, keeping stakeholders informed.
- **Results**: concrete, measured outcomes and what you would do differently.

## Common variants

- "What is the project you are most proud of?"
- "Walk me through the most complex initiative you have driven."
- "Tell me about a time you delivered something with many moving parts."
- "Describe a project where you were the technical lead. What did leading mean in practice?"
