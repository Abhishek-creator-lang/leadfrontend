> "Tell me about a technical decision you made that, looking back, you'd make differently. What happened, and what would you do now?"

Leads make decisions that other people live with for years. Interviewers want to see that you can own a decision that aged badly, reason about why it went wrong without blaming the team or circumstances, and show that it changed how you decide.

## What the interviewer is assessing

- **Ownership**: do you say "I decided" and own the consequences?
- **Decision quality**: was the original decision reasonable given what you knew, and can you separate a bad outcome from a bad process?
- **Detection**: how and when did you notice it was wrong? Did you have signals in place?
- **Remediation**: what did you do about it, and at what cost?
- **Learning that sticks**: a concrete change to how you make decisions now, not a vague lesson.

## Common variants

- "What's an architecture choice you regret?"
- "Tell me about a time you were wrong about a technology."
- "What technical debt have you personally created?"
- "If you could restart your current codebase, what would you change?"
