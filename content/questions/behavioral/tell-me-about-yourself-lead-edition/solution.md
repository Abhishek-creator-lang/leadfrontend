## How to structure your answer

This is not a STAR story. Use a **Present, Past, Future** pitch and aim for 90 seconds to two minutes.

1. **Present (40%)**: your current role in one sentence of scope (team size, product, what you own), then one or two headline outcomes with numbers.
2. **Past (35%)**: two or three career beats that explain how you got here. Each beat should add a capability: deep front-end skill, then architecture, then leading people.
3. **Future (25%)**: what you want next and why this role fits. Tie it to something specific about the company or team.

End with a hook the interviewer can pick up, such as the project you are most proud of. Prepare a 30-second and a three-minute version too, because some interviewers cut in early and others let you run.

## Model answer

"I'm a front-end lead at a B2B analytics company, where I lead six engineers who own our customer-facing dashboard product. It's a React and TypeScript app used by about 40,000 analysts a day, and it's heavy on data grids and charts.

Over the last year, the two things I'm proudest of are these. First, I led the move from a client-only single-page app to server rendering with streaming. That took our p75 Largest Contentful Paint from 4.1 seconds to 1.9, and support tickets about 'slow dashboards' fell by about 60 percent. Second, I built our team's on-call and release practice. We went from a weekly release with a manual QA checklist to continuous deploys behind feature flags, around 15 deploys a week, with a lower change-failure rate than before.

How I got here: I started as a UI engineer at a media company, building ad-supported article pages. That's where I learned to care about performance budgets, because every 100 milliseconds showed up in ad revenue. Then I joined a fintech startup as the second front-end hire. With a tiny team I ended up owning the component library, the build tooling and accessibility, and that's where I moved from writing features to making decisions other engineers build on. When we grew, I was asked to lead the web team, and I found I enjoy multiplying a team more than shipping alone.

What I'm looking for now is a role where I set front-end direction across several teams, not just one. Your platform is moving to a shared design system and a monorepo, which is exactly the kind of cross-team problem I want to own next. I'd be happy to go deeper on the server-rendering migration, since it covers most of how I work as a lead."

## Why this answer works

- **Scope first**: team size, users and product in the opening sentence set a lead-level frame immediately.
- **Outcomes with baselines**: LCP 4.1 s to 1.9 s and 60 percent fewer tickets, rather than a list of technologies.
- **Both technical and organisational impact**: a performance migration and a delivery practice change.
- **Coherent arc**: each past role adds a capability (performance, platform ownership, leading people), so the story explains why they are a lead.
- **Specific motivation**: names something real about the target team rather than "I like your culture".
- **Ends with a hook**: steers the next question toward a prepared strong story.

## Red flags to avoid

- Reciting your resume in date order from university onward. The interviewer has already read it.
- Listing tools ("React, Redux, Webpack, Jest…") instead of impact.
- Going past three minutes, or giving a 20-second answer that offers nothing to follow up on.
- Personal life details that do not relate to the role.
- Negative comments about your current employer as the reason for the move.
- Describing yourself only as an individual contributor when applying for a lead role. Show that you lead people and decisions.

## Likely follow-up probes

- **"Tell me more about the server-rendering migration."** Have a full STAR-L story ready. Expect questions on hydration cost, caching and how you sequenced the rollout.
- **"What does a typical week look like for you?"** Give a realistic split, for example 40 percent hands-on code and reviews, 30 percent planning and design reviews, 30 percent one-on-ones and stakeholders. It shows you understand what a lead actually does.
- **"Why leave now?"** Answer with what you are moving toward (wider scope), not what you are running from.
- **"What would your team say about you?"** Quote real feedback, including one area you are working on.
- **"Are you more interested in the staff track or the management track?"** Be honest about your preference and explain what draws you to it.
