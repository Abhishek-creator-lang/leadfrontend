## How to structure your answer

Use a short **Appreciate – Reason – Towards** structure, about 45–60 seconds:

1. **Appreciate**: one sentence on what you gained or are proud of in the current role. This signals you are not running away.
2. **Reason**: the honest, neutral reason. Make it about the role's shape or direction, not about people.
3. **Towards**: what you want next, and how this role provides it. This should connect to your answer for "why us".

Rules of thumb:

- Never lie. Reference checks and later questions will expose it. You can choose *which* true reason to lead with.
- Keep it short. A long explanation sounds defensive.
- If the real reason is a bad manager or culture, describe the *need* it created ("I do my best work where technical decisions are made in writing and openly"), not the person.

## Model answer

**Standard case: growth.**

"I have been at my current company for almost four years and I am proud of what we built. I joined as a senior engineer when the web team was four people and I now lead seven. We took our self-serve onboarding from a jQuery app to a typed React codebase, and moved our deploys from weekly to several times a day behind feature flags.

The reason I am looking is that the front-end scope there has stabilised. The company is focused on the back-end platform for the next couple of years, and the front-end work ahead is mostly maintenance and incremental features. I have talked openly with my manager about this, and we agree there is no lead role with broader architecture scope coming soon.

What I am looking for is exactly what this role describes: leading front-end architecture across several teams, with a design system and a performance programme to drive. That is where I want to spend the next few years."

**Tricky case: layoff.**

"My role was eliminated in a restructuring in March, along with about 20% of engineering. The product line I worked on was closed. Before that, I led the migration of our customer portal to Next.js, which cut p75 LCP from 3.8 to 2.1 seconds. Since then I have been doing some open-source work on an accessible combobox component while being selective about my next role, because I want a lead position where front end is central to the product, which is why this one interests me."

**Tricky case: short tenure.**

"I joined my current company 11 months ago to lead a new web app. Six months in, the company changed strategy to mobile-only and the web team was reduced to maintenance. I delivered the launch and handed it over cleanly with documentation and runbooks, but the role I joined for no longer exists. I would rather say that openly than stay in a job I am not engaged in."

## Why this answer works

- **Starts with achievement**, so the move reads as growth rather than escape.
- **Neutral, structural reason**: company direction, role scope or a layoff, with no blame.
- **Shows maturity**: the candidate already raised it with their manager, which shows they try to fix things before leaving.
- **Points directly at this role**, linking to "why us".
- **Handles hard cases calmly**: layoffs and short tenures are stated as facts, with evidence of good work and a clean handover.

## Red flags to avoid

- Criticising your manager, team or company, even if it is true and justified.
- "Money" as the only reason. Compensation can be part of it, but it should not lead.
- A reason that also applies to this company. If you say you want a small company and this is a large one, you have a problem.
- Vague or evasive answers that make the interviewer dig.
- Over-explaining a layoff or a short tenure, which makes it sound like there is more to it.
- Saying you have not discussed your goals with your current employer at all, which suggests you avoid hard conversations.

## Likely follow-up probes

- **"What would make you stay?"** Be honest. If a matching lead role appeared, you might consider it, but it is unlikely given the company's direction. Avoid sounding as if you are only using this process for a counter-offer.
- **"Did you try to change things internally?"** Give a specific example of a conversation or proposal, and its outcome.
- **"How would your manager describe you?"** Have two or three phrases you would genuinely expect, ideally the same ones your references will use.
- **"Why were you chosen for the layoff?"** Say that whole product lines or teams were closed, if true, and that it was not performance-based. Offer references.
- **"You have moved jobs three times in four years. Why?"** Explain each move briefly and show the thread connecting them, then explain why you are looking for a longer stay now.
