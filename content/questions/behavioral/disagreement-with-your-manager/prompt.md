> "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager. How did you handle it?"

Disagreeing upward is harder than disagreeing with a peer, because the manager can simply decide. Interviewers want to see that you raise concerns clearly and early, in the right setting, with a recommendation, and that you commit fully when the decision goes the other way.

## What the interviewer is assessing

- **Courage**: will you speak up to people with more authority?
- **Judgement about the setting**: private first, written when needed, never undermining in public.
- **Quality of the argument**: risk, cost and alternatives, framed around shared goals.
- **Disagree and commit**: do you support the final decision with your team?
- **Follow-through**: did you revisit with data later, without saying "I told you so"?

## Common variants

- "Describe a time you challenged a decision from leadership."
- "Tell me about a time you were asked to do something you thought was wrong."
- "Have you ever had to tell your team about a decision you didn't agree with?"
- "When did you last push back on your boss?"
