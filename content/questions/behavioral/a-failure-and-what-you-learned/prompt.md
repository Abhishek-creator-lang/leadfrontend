> "Tell me about a time you failed. Not a near miss: something that actually went wrong and was at least partly your fault. What happened, and what did you take away from it?"

Almost every loop includes some version of this question. At senior and lead level the bar is higher: the failure should have real stakes, you should own your part without beating yourself up, and the lesson should show up as a lasting change in how you or your team works.

## What the interviewer is assessing

- **Self-awareness**: can you name your own mistake clearly, without blaming others or circumstances?
- **Stakes and scope**: is the failure big enough to be meaningful for someone at your level?
- **Recovery**: what you did once you realised things were going wrong, including how you communicated.
- **Learning that sticks**: a concrete change in behaviour or process, ideally with evidence it worked later.
- **Psychological safety**: how you talk about failure tells them how you will treat failure on your team.

## Common variants

- "Tell me about a project that did not go the way you planned."
- "What is the biggest mistake you have made in your career?"
- "Describe a decision you made that turned out to be wrong."
- "Tell me about a time you missed a commitment."
