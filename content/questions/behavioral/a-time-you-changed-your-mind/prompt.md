> "Tell me about a time you were convinced you were right about a technical decision, and then changed your mind. What changed it?"

Leads are expected to have strong opinions, but also to update them when the evidence changes. This question tests intellectual honesty and how you handle being wrong in front of a team.

## What the interviewer is assessing

- **Openness to evidence**: what kind of argument or data actually moves you.
- **Ego management**: can you reverse a position you argued for publicly without losing credibility?
- **How you seek disconfirming information**: do you wait to be proven wrong, or go looking?
- **Handling the reversal**: communicating the change, and dealing with work already done.
- **Psychological safety**: whether your behaviour makes it safe for others to disagree with you.

## Common variants

- "Tell me about a time someone more junior convinced you that you were wrong."
- "Describe a strongly held opinion you no longer hold."
- "When was the last time you were wrong about something important at work?"
- "How do you decide when to hold your position and when to let go?"
