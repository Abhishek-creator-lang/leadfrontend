> "How do you see the difference between a tech lead, a staff engineer and an engineering manager? Which one are you, and where do you want to be in three years?"

Companies define these roles differently, and hiring the wrong shape of leader is expensive. The interviewer wants to see that you understand the trade-offs and that your choice is based on what you enjoy and are good at, not on title.

## What the interviewer is assessing

- **Understanding of the roles**: scope, responsibilities and how success is measured for each.
- **Self-knowledge**: evidence from your own work about where you add the most value.
- **Fit with this role**: does your preference match what the team actually needs?
- **Maturity about career**: you see the tracks as different jobs, not a ladder.
- **Flexibility**: can you do some of the other roles' work when the team needs it?

## Common variants

- "Do you want to be a manager?"
- "What does a staff engineer do that a senior engineer does not?"
- "How much do you want to code in this role?"
- "What part of leading do you enjoy least?"
