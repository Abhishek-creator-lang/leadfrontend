## How to structure your answer

This is a scenario, not a story. Use **Prepare – Defer – Anchor – Trade**:

1. **Prepare**: research ranges for the level and location from salary surveys, public pay-transparency postings, peer networks and level-comparison sites. Know your walk-away number and your priorities (base, equity, level, remote, title).
2. **Defer**: early in the process, ask for the role's band and say you want to understand the level and full scope first. In many places, employers cannot legally ask your current salary, and you do not have to share it.
3. **Anchor**: when you must give a number, give a researched range for *this* level, with your target near the bottom of the range so the low end is still acceptable.
4. **Trade**: negotiate on the whole package and on level using evidence, and always tie requests to something specific.

Laws and norms vary by country and state, so check local pay-transparency rules before your first recruiter call.

## Model answer

**Scenario 1: early salary question from a recruiter.**

"Thanks for asking. Before I give a number, could you share the band for this role? I would also like to confirm the level, since the posting says 'lead' and I want to make sure we mean the same scope: leading front-end architecture across several squads.

*(The recruiter asks again for a number.)*

Understood. Based on what I have researched for lead-level front-end roles in this city and similar company stages, I am looking at a total compensation range of 165 to 185 thousand, with flexibility depending on the equity and the level. I am more focused on getting the scope and level right than on maximising base salary."

**Scenario 2: down-level after the final round.**

"Thank you, I am glad the panel was positive, and I am still very interested. Before I respond on the level, could you share what feedback led to senior rather than lead? I want to understand the gap.

*(The recruiter says the system design round showed strong depth but less evidence of cross-team leadership.)*

That is fair feedback, and it may be that I did not bring out the right examples in that round. In my current role I set direction for front-end architecture across three teams. For example, I wrote and drove the RFC for our move to a shared Vite-based monorepo, which cut CI time from 22 to 8 minutes and was adopted by all three teams. I also run our front-end guild and have grown one senior engineer into a lead. I would be happy to do a short follow-up conversation with the hiring manager on cross-team leadership if that would help.

If the level stays at senior, I would want to agree on two things: compensation at the top of the senior band, and a written plan for a level review at six months with clear criteria. Would that be possible?"

**Scenario 3: competing offer.**

"I want to be transparent: I have another offer at the lead level with a total compensation of 180. This role is still my first choice because of the design system work and the team. If you can get closer to that number, or match the level, I am ready to accept this week."

## Why this answer works

- **Asks for the band first** without being evasive, then gives a researched range when pressed.
- **Prioritises level and scope**, showing long-term thinking.
- **Treats down-leveling as information**: asks for feedback, responds with evidence, offers a follow-up.
- **Has a fallback**: top of band plus a written six-month level review.
- **Uses competing offers honestly** and commits to a decision if terms are met, which recruiters value.
- **Polite and collaborative throughout**; the recruiter is a partner trying to close the hire.

## Red flags to avoid

- Naming a number before you know the level or have researched the range.
- Sharing your current salary when you do not have to, which anchors the offer to your past rather than the role.
- Inventing or exaggerating competing offers. It is unethical and can end the process.
- Accepting a down-level without understanding why, or rejecting it angrily.
- Negotiating only on base salary while ignoring equity, signing bonus, level, title and start date.
- Making demands with no reason attached, or negotiating after you have already said yes.

## Likely follow-up probes

- **"Is that number flexible?"** Say the package matters more than any single part, and ask which parts they have flexibility on.
- **"Can you give me your minimum?"** Do not. Restate your range and the factors that would move you within it.
- **"Why should we level you higher than the panel recommended?"** Give two or three pieces of evidence of scope at the higher level and offer a focused follow-up interview.
- **"We cannot move on salary."** Ask about signing bonus, equity, an earlier compensation review, extra learning budget, or flexible working.
- **"Can you decide by Friday?"** If you need more time, ask for a specific short extension and explain why. Most companies agree to a reasonable one.
