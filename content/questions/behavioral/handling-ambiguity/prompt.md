> "Tell me about a time you were given a goal with very little definition: no spec, unclear owners, maybe unclear success criteria. What did you do?"

The higher the level, the less defined the work. Interviewers want to see that you create clarity for yourself and others, make reasonable assumptions, and move forward while reducing uncertainty, instead of waiting for someone to hand you a spec.

## What the interviewer is assessing

- **Problem framing**: turning a vague goal into a specific question with success criteria.
- **Bias for action**: making progress through prototypes, spikes and experiments.
- **Explicit assumptions**: writing them down and checking them.
- **Stakeholder management**: getting the right people to agree on scope and definition of done.
- **Comfort with change**: adjusting when new information arrives.

## Common variants

- "Describe a project where the requirements kept changing."
- "How do you start work when nobody can tell you exactly what they want?"
- "Tell me about a time you had to make a decision without all the information."
- "How do you handle a vague request from leadership?"
