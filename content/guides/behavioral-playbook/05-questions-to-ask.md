"Do you have any questions for me?" isn't a formality. It's part of the evaluation, and it's your best chance to find out whether you actually want the job. Good questions signal seniority and genuine interest. Their answers give you information for choosing between offers and for negotiating. This chapter gives you a question bank organized by interviewer and goal.

## Principles

- **Prepare two or three questions per interviewer,** tailored to their role. Have backups in case earlier rounds answered them.
- **Ask open questions** that invite stories: "Tell me about…", "How did…", "What happened when…".
- **Follow up on the answer.** A follow-up shows you listened and gets you to the real information.
- **Don't ask what's on the careers page.** It signals low effort.
- **Avoid compensation and leave questions in technical rounds.** Save them for the recruiter.
- **Different interviewers, same question:** asking two people the same question (e.g. about decision-making) and comparing answers is very revealing.

## Questions by interviewer role

### Peer engineer

| Question | What it reveals |
| --- | --- |
| "What does a typical week look like for you: coding, reviews, meetings, support?" | Real focus time and toil |
| "What's the most interesting front end problem you've worked on here in the last six months?" | Technical depth and challenge level |
| "How long does it take from merging a PR to it being in production?" | Delivery maturity |
| "What's something about the codebase you'd change if you could?" | Tech debt and candor |
| "How are on-call and production issues handled for the front end?" | Operational load and maturity |
| "How do code reviews work? How long do PRs usually wait?" | Collaboration and velocity |

### Hiring manager

| Question | What it reveals |
| --- | --- |
| "What would make someone in this role clearly successful after six months? After a year?" | Expectations and level |
| "What's the biggest challenge the team faces right now?" | Where you'd spend your energy |
| "Why is this role open?" | Growth vs backfill; turnover |
| "How do you support engineers' growth? Who on the team was recently promoted, and how?" | Career development reality |
| "How do you handle disagreements between engineering and product?" | Decision culture |
| "How is performance evaluated, and how often?" | Process clarity |
| "How would you describe your management style? What do your reports say about you?" | Fit with you |

### Staff/lead peer or architect

| Question | What it reveals |
| --- | --- |
| "How are cross-team technical decisions made and recorded?" | RFC/ADR culture, influence model |
| "What's the front end architecture's biggest weakness, and is there a plan for it?" | Honest technical picture; where you'd lead |
| "What does the staff/lead engineer here own that nobody else does?" | Role scope and expectations |
| "How is platform or tech-debt work prioritized against product work?" | Investment in quality |
| "Tell me about a technical decision that was reversed. What happened?" | Learning culture, ego |

### Product manager or designer

| Question | What it reveals |
| --- | --- |
| "At what stage are engineers involved in shaping features?" | Engineering influence on product |
| "How do you decide what to build next? What data do you use?" | Product maturity |
| "What's a recent time engineering pushed back and changed the plan?" | Whether pushback is welcome |
| "How do design and engineering collaborate on the design system?" | Craft and consistency |
| "How do you measure whether a feature succeeded?" | Outcome focus |

### Director/VP or skip-level

| Question | What it reveals |
| --- | --- |
| "What are the engineering org's top priorities for the next year?" | Strategy and alignment |
| "How does front end fit into the company's technical strategy?" | Front end's status and investment |
| "What's the biggest risk to the org's plans?" | Candor and awareness |
| "How do you see this role evolving in two years?" | Growth path |
| "What do you look for when promoting someone to staff?" | Real promotion criteria |

### Recruiter

| Question | What it reveals |
| --- | --- |
| "What level is this role, and how is level decided?" | Leveling mechanics |
| "What's the full interview process and timeline?" | Planning |
| "What's the compensation range for this level?" | Negotiation baseline (many jurisdictions require ranges to be disclosed) |
| "Is there team matching after the offer?" | Process |
| "What feedback have past candidates found most useful for preparing?" | Prep focus |

## Questions by goal

### To assess engineering health

- "What's your deploy frequency and how do you roll back?"
- "How do you know when the front end is broken in production?"
- "What's your approach to testing? What runs in CI?"
- "How are accessibility and performance treated: requirements, nice-to-haves, or someone's side project?"

### To assess culture and sustainability

- "When was the last time the team worked late or on a weekend? What led to it?"
- "How are mistakes handled? Can you describe a recent postmortem?"
- "How are remote and in-office team members included?"
- "What's something the team did recently to improve how it works?"

### To assess your potential impact

- "If I joined, what's the first problem you'd want me to tackle?"
- "What's one thing that would make this team noticeably better that nobody has had time to do?"
- "Where does the team need more senior leadership: technical direction, mentoring, process?"

### To close strong (end of a good round)

- "Based on our conversation, is there anything that makes you hesitant about my fit for this role?"

Use this one carefully. It gives you a chance to address a concern immediately, but it can feel awkward. It works best with hiring managers. Respond to any concern calmly with specific evidence, without arguing.

## Listening for red flags

| Signal in the answer | Possible concern |
| --- | --- |
| Vague answers about success criteria | Unclear expectations; hard to succeed or get promoted |
| "We're too busy for tests/docs/refactoring" | Chronic firefighting |
| Different interviewers describe different priorities | Misalignment, possible re-orgs |
| Nobody can name a recent promotion | Limited growth |
| Heroics celebrated ("she stayed up all night to fix it") | Unsustainable culture |
| Blame in incident stories | Not psychologically safe |
| "The previous person left suddenly" without context | Possible management issues |
| Front end described as "just the UI layer" | Low investment and influence |

None of these is a dealbreaker on its own, but patterns across interviewers matter.

## Green flags

- Specific, recent examples in answers.
- Interviewers admit weaknesses and describe plans to fix them.
- Engineers describe influencing product decisions.
- Clear, written processes for decisions, incidents, and promotions.
- Consistent answers across interviewers.

## Note-taking and follow-up

- Write down the answers right after each round. They're easy to blur across five interviews.
- Use them to compare offers (team health, growth, scope) beyond compensation.
- In negotiation, refer to what you learned: "Given the scope of leading the design system migration we discussed, I believe this role is better matched to staff level."

## Quick list: top ten all-purpose questions

1. What would make someone clearly successful in this role after six months?
2. What's the biggest challenge facing the team right now?
3. How are technical decisions made and recorded?
4. What happens from PR merge to production, and how long does it take?
5. How do you know when the front end is broken for users?
6. What's something you'd change about the codebase or process?
7. How involved are engineers in shaping product decisions?
8. Who was promoted recently, and what made the case work?
9. Tell me about a recent incident and what changed afterwards.
10. Is there anything about my background that makes you hesitant?
