Your level is decided during the interview process, and it affects compensation, scope, and promotion timelines for years. Yet most candidates treat leveling as something that just happens to them. This chapter explains how hiring pipelines and leveling actually work, so you can steer both.

## The pipeline

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  Source[Application or referral] --> Screen[Recruiter screen]
  Screen --> Tech[Technical screen]
  Tech --> Loop[Interview loop]
  Loop --> Debrief[Debrief or committee]
  Debrief --> Level[Level and comp approval]
  Level --> Offer[Offer]
  Offer --> Match[Team match if applicable]
```

| Stage | Who decides | What you can influence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sourcing | Recruiter / hiring manager | Referrals greatly increase response rates; target the right role level |
| Recruiter screen | Recruiter | Initial level bucket; logistics; your narrative |
| Technical screen | Engineer | Pass/fail plus early level signal |
| Loop | 4–6 interviewers | Evidence per competency |
| Debrief / committee | Hiring manager, committee, sometimes bar raiser | You can't attend, but your interviewers' notes speak for you |
| Comp approval | Recruiter + comp team | Competing offers, level justification |
| Team match | Hiring managers | Your preferences and conversations |

### Referrals

A referral from a current employee typically moves your application to the front of the queue. Ask former colleagues, give them a short blurb and your resume, and specify the role and level you're targeting.

## What a "level" is

Levels define expected **scope, autonomy, and impact**, and set compensation bands.

| Generic level | Typical years* | Scope | Autonomy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entry / Junior | 0–2 | Tasks | Guided |
| Mid | 2–5 | Features | Independent on defined work |
| Senior | 5–8+ | Projects, product area | Independent on ambiguous work; guides others |
| Staff / Lead | 8+ | Multiple teams, platform | Sets direction for an area |
| Senior Staff / Principal | 12+ | Org or company domain | Sets direction across orgs |

*Years are only a rough correlation. Scope and demonstrated impact decide the level.

### Rough cross-company mapping

Titles don't transfer cleanly between companies. A rough equivalence often used by candidates (from public leveling comparisons):

| Generic | Google | Meta | Amazon | Microsoft |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mid | L4 | E4 | SDE II (L5) | 61–62 |
| Senior | L5 | E5 | SDE III / Senior (L6) | 63–64 |
| Staff | L6 | E6 | Principal (L7) | 65–66 |
| Senior Staff | L7 | E7 | Senior Principal (L8) | 67 |

Treat this as approximate. Leveling sites like levels.fyi aggregate self-reported data and are useful for comparisons. At startups, "Senior" or "Lead" can mean anything from mid-level to staff. Ask about scope, not titles.

## How level is decided

1. **Initial target:** set by the recruiter from your resume and screen. It decides which interview loop and bar you get.
2. **Loop calibration:** each interviewer gives a hire/no-hire recommendation **and** often a level signal.
3. **Debrief/committee:** evidence is weighed. Strong design and behavioral signals push up; weak coding pulls down.
4. **Outcomes:** hire at target level, hire at a level below (down-level), or no hire. Up-levels happen but are rarer.

### Why down-leveling happens

- Behavioral stories at a smaller scope than the target level.
- System design without trade-offs, operations, or team-boundary thinking.
- Mixed signals between interviewers.
- Being interviewed on the wrong loop (e.g. a senior loop when targeting staff).

## Influencing your level

| When | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Before applying | Target roles that match the scope you've demonstrated; get referrers to mention your level |
| Recruiter screen | State the level you're targeting and why, with scope evidence: "I lead front end architecture across four teams…" |
| Before the loop | Ask: "Which level is the loop calibrated for? Is there a separate staff loop?" |
| During the loop | Produce level-appropriate evidence (scope, trade-offs, leverage) in every round |
| After a down-level offer | Ask for the specific feedback; provide extra evidence; ask about a follow-up interview for the higher level; or negotiate comp within the level |

Some companies will run one extra interview (often a system design or leadership round) to reconsider the level if you push back with evidence. It doesn't hurt to ask politely.

### Is a down-level ever the right choice?

Sometimes. Consider:

| Factor | Accept down-level | Decline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comp | Still a significant increase | Below your current total comp |
| Promotion path | Clear, with a realistic 12–18 month timeline | Vague or slow |
| Company/brand/learning | Exceptional opportunity | Comparable to alternatives |
| Scope | Real work matches your higher level, so promotion evidence comes naturally | Scope is genuinely smaller |

## Timelines

| Company type | Typical duration (application → offer) |
| --- | --- |
| Big tech | 4–8 weeks (longer with team matching) |
| Scale-up | 2–5 weeks |
| Startup | 1–3 weeks |

Plan to run several processes in parallel so your offers arrive close together. Ask recruiters to adjust schedules (delay or speed up) to line up timing. They do this routinely.

## Hiring committees and bar raisers

- **Committees** review written feedback without having met you. Clear, quotable evidence in each round matters.
- **Bar raisers** (or "independent assessors") are trained interviewers from outside the hiring team who can veto. Their focus is the long-term bar and core values.
- **Hiring manager influence** varies: decisive at startups, one input among several at big companies.

## Internal mobility and promotions vs external hiring

External moves often reset or raise your level faster than internal promotion, but they also carry risk (ramping up, losing internal reputation). Compare:

| Path | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal promotion | Reputation and context carry over | Slower; dependent on cycle and budget |
| External move at level | Fresh start, potentially higher comp | Ramp-up; must rebuild trust |
| External move with level-up | Fastest level gain | Hard to land; higher expectations immediately |

## Questions to ask about leveling

- "Which level is this role, and what scope does that level have on this team?"
- "How many levels does the engineering ladder have, and is there a published ladder?"
- "What distinguishes senior from staff here, concretely?"
- "What's the typical time to promotion from this level for strong performers?"
- "If the loop suggests a different level, how is that handled?"

## Checklist

- [ ] I know the level I'm targeting, and my evidence for it
- [ ] I've stated my target level to the recruiter with scope examples
- [ ] I know which level the loop is calibrated for
- [ ] My stories and designs show level-appropriate scope
- [ ] I've lined up parallel processes to align offer timing
- [ ] I know how I'd respond to a down-level offer
